About

I am a Research Master's student in History and Philosophy of Science and Logic at Ruhr University Bochum. My background spans philosophy, logic, theology, and physics.

I am interested in the epistemology of mathematics and logic, formal and social epistemology, non-classical logics, and the use of formal methods in philosophy. I also have broader interests in the history of mathematics and logic and in Chinese and Japanese intellectual history.

Research interests

  • Philosophy of mathematics and logic. Epistemological questions concerning mathematical knowledge, logical consequence, and the norms of formal reasoning.
  • Methodology of philosophy. Formal methods, including logic for modelling, agent-based modelling, quantitative methods, and argumentation.
  • Formal and social epistemology. Epistemic logic, epistemic utility theory, expertise, echo chambers, and the epistemology of games.
  • Non-classical logic. Intuitionistic and many-valued logics and their philosophical applications.
  • History of ideas. Twentieth-century history of mathematics and logic, and Chinese and Japanese intellectual history.

Mathematical Definitions: Inquiry, Authority, and Mathematical Practice

Presentation at the 8th Masterclass in the Philosophy of Mathematical Practices with Silvia De Toffoli · Vrije Universiteit Brussel

Abstract. Mathematical definitions do more than stipulate meanings: they can guide proofs, sharpen concepts, and acquire authority within mathematical practice. This talk compares the stipulative conception of definition with Lakatos's account of proof-generated definitions, suggesting that design and discovery are compatible aspects of mathematical inquiry. It sketches a pragmatic model in which a mathematician proposes a definition for a specific purpose, others recognize and assess the proposal, and its adoption makes it part of a group's common ground. The model aims to explain how definitions become operative standards in particular contexts while remaining open to revision. It leaves open which norms justify their wider acceptance and whether formal pragmatics or adaptive logic can help articulate them.
